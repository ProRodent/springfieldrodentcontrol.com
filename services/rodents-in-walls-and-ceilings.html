Rodents in Walls and Ceilings in Springfield, MO

Scratching in a wall at night, or something running across the ceiling, points to a route you can close.

Call (844) 635-0403 Contact Us

Noise in the walls is how many people first learn they have rodents. They describe it as running in the ceiling, scratching at night or something moving between floors.

What the noises tell us

University of Missouri Extension says rodents are mostly active at night and lists droppings, gnawing, rub marks and a musky odor as signs. Noise that moves between the attic and the walls usually means a route along framing, pipes or wires.

Finding how they get in

Mice climb rough vertical surfaces and fit through gaps about a quarter inch wide, so a route to the ceiling can start at a vent, a utility line or the roofline. We trace likely entry points and decide where traps should go before anything is opened.

Trapping and access

Traps go where activity is heard or seen, close to walls and in dark corners. Extension advises against indoor rodenticides because animals can die inside walls and cause odor, so we rely on traps and sealing. If a trap line cannot reach the activity, we may recommend a small access opening.

Wall and ceiling work includes

  • Listening and inspecting for the route between attic and walls
  • Checking vents, utility lines and the roofline for gaps
  • Trap placement near the activity
  • Sealing entry points after trapping
  • Notes on droppings, gnawing and insulation

FAQs About Rodents in Walls and Ceilings in Springfield

Can you get rats out of a wall without tearing it open?

Often, yes, by trapping at entry points and in the spaces on either side. If that fails, we explain the options before any opening is made.

Why are droppings showing up from the ceiling?

Animals in the attic or between floors can drop material through gaps around fixtures and vents. The source is usually above, so we inspect the attic too.

Is the noise a mouse or a rat?

Hard to tell by sound alone. Droppings, gnawing and rub marks help us identify which animal is present.

Should I put poison in the wall?

We advise against it. Extension warns that animals can die in the wall and cause odor.
