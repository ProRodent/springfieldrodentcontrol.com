Rodent Droppings Cleanup in Springfield, MO

Sweeping or vacuuming droppings can put particles in the air. Cleanup has to be wet, careful and thorough.

Call (844) 635-0403 Contact Us

Rodents contaminate surfaces with urine, droppings and saliva. Cleaning those up is not the same as ordinary housework, and doing it wrong can spread the problem.

What CDC advises

CDC says not to vacuum or sweep rodent urine, droppings or nesting material, because that can release droplets into the air. The agency recommends airing the space for 30 minutes, wearing rubber or plastic gloves, wetting droppings with disinfectant such as 1.5 cups of bleach in 1 gallon of water, waiting 5 minutes, and wiping up with paper towels.

Disposal

CDC advises bagging paper towels and dead rodents, sealing the bag, placing it inside a second bag and putting it in a covered garbage can that is emptied regularly. Heavy infestations call for added precautions.

When to call us

If droppings are in an attic, crawl space or inside insulation, cleanup is harder and may require removing material. We assess the area, tell you what can be cleaned and what should be replaced, and coordinate with insulation work where needed.

Droppings in the places people find them

Droppings turn up behind baseboards, under the oven, in cabinets near the stove, in the basement and across attic insulation. Extension says mouse droppings look like dark pieces of rice and Norway rat droppings are larger. Tell us what you found and where, and whether you want an evaluation only or cleanup too.

Cleanup steps

  • Ventilate the area before work begins
  • Wear gloves and avoid stirring dust
  • Wet droppings and nesting material with disinfectant
  • Wipe up with disposable towels
  • Bag, double-bag and dispose of waste

FAQs About Rodent Droppings Cleanup in Springfield

Can I clean droppings myself?

For a small amount in a kitchen cabinet, yes, following CDC steps. For attics, crawl spaces or heavy contamination, call us.

Is a vacuum okay?

No. CDC advises against vacuuming or sweeping droppings and nesting materials.

What about cabinets and pantry items?

Food that was exposed should usually be thrown away. Hard surfaces can be disinfected, and porous items may need to go.

How do I know the problem is over?

Fresh droppings stop appearing, traps stop catching and the entry points are sealed. Cleanup is best done after that.

Can you tell what animal left these droppings?

Usually. Size and location help, and gnawing, rub marks and nests during the inspection confirm it.

How do I clean a trap I want to reuse?

CDC says reusable snap traps can be soaked in disinfectant for 5 minutes, rinsed and dried. Wear gloves, and wash your hands with soap and water afterward.
