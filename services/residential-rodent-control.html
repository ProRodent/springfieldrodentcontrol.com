Residential Rodent Control in Springfield, MO

For houses, duplexes and condos: inspect the whole structure, treat the animals and close the routes in.

Call (844) 635-0403 Contact Us

Most home rodent calls start with a noise in the attic, a trail of droppings in a cabinet or a chewed bag in the garage. A home visit works through the building from the outside in.

Outside first

Walking the exterior shows the gaps around utility lines, dryer vents, garage door seals and foundation vents. University of Missouri Extension recommends caulk around windows, doors, dryer vents and electrical and gas lines, with copper wool packed into larger openings.

Attic, crawl space and living areas

Inside, we check the attic for droppings and nesting material, the crawl space or basement for burrow entrances and runways, and the kitchen and garage for gnawing. Each space tells us something different about which species is present.

Keeping the house less appealing

Rodents need food, water and shelter. Sealed pantry containers, pet food put away at night, trash in lidded bins and a clear gap between woodpiles and walls remove the reasons to stay.

Different rooms, different clues

A mouse in a bedroom, droppings in a cabinet beside the stove or sounds in the ceiling at night each point to a different route. If you have just moved in or are buying a house, say so on the call so the first inspection can focus on the attic, crawl space and garage.

Maintenance plans for homes

After the first job, some homeowners keep a maintenance plan to check traps and sealed areas through the year, especially after a bad fall or winter. Ask what a plan would include for your house.

Home visit checklist

  • Exterior walk-around and gap check
  • Attic and crawl space inspection
  • Kitchen, garage and utility room check
  • Trap and bait station placement
  • Written list of repairs and habits to change

If this matches what you are seeing, it may also help to read about rodent inspection, rodent exclusion and proofing the attic.

Source for the facts about rodent control for the home on this page: University of Missouri Extension, Prevent mice and rats from coming inside (November 2025).

FAQs About Residential Rodent Control in Springfield

Do I need to leave during treatment?

Usually not. Traps are placed out of the way, and bait stations, if used, are tamper-resistant and placed along outside walls.

How soon should I call after seeing droppings?

Soon. A small problem is faster and cheaper in effort to resolve than one that has had a breeding season to expand.

Do you handle garages and sheds?

Yes. Detached buildings are common rodent shelters and sometimes the start of a house problem.

Will you tell me what to seal?

Yes. You get a written list of entry points, and we can do the sealing when you want it done.

Is treatment safe for dogs and cats?

Indoors we use snap traps placed along walls and out of pet reach where possible, and bait only in tamper-resistant stations. Tell us what pets you have so we can plan placement.

Do you treat mobile homes and cabins?

Yes. Underpinning, skirting and log construction each have their own gaps, so tell us the type of building when you call.

Do you offer a yearly or ongoing plan?

Yes, we offer maintenance plans for homes. Ask on the call what a plan would include for your property.

What should I store differently?

Seal garbage cans, birdseed, livestock feed and pet food, and clear brush and woodpiles from the foundation. Extension lists these as steps that reduce food and shelter for rodents.
