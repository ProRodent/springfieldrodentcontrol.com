Rodent Exclusion Services in Springfield, MO

Trapping clears the animals that are inside. Exclusion stops the next ones from coming in.

Call (844) 635-0403 Contact Us

Exclusion means closing the routes rodents use to enter a building. It is the part of rodent control that lasts, because it fixes the building as well as clearing the animals.

How big a gap is a problem

University of Missouri Extension says mice can enter holes about one quarter inch across and rats can gnaw openings as small as half an inch. Rat burrow entrances can be about 2 inches across. Those figures drive how we measure and what we seal.

What works and what does not

Extension recommends caulk around windows, doors, dryer vents and electrical and gas lines, with copper wool stuffed into larger openings because rodents cannot chew through it. Plastic, wood and cloth will not hold. Screens without holes make good barriers.

Order of work matters

Sealing before trapping can lock animals inside. We usually start with trapping and monitoring, then close gaps once activity falls, so the animals still in the building have a way out and none come back in.

Winterizing before cold weather

Many people call in fall to get the house winterized: plumbing and utility openings, gas line gaps and the foundation line sealed before mice move in for winter. You can book exclusion even if you have not seen a mouse yet.

Follow-up and maintenance

Sealing holds best when it is checked over time. We offer maintenance plans that revisit trap lines and sealed entry points, and you can ask about one when you book exclusion.

Exclusion work includes

  • Measuring gaps around pipes, vents, doors and the foundation line
  • Caulk and copper wool in small and mid-size openings
  • Screen or metal repair for vents and larger holes
  • Door sweep and garage seal checks
  • Written record of each gap sealed

Related pages cover rat proofing, mouse proofing and sealing of entry points and proofing the attic for situations that go beyond this one.

Source for the facts about rodent exclusion on this page: University of Missouri Extension, Prevent mice and rats from coming inside (November 2025).

FAQs About Rodent Exclusion Services in Springfield

How do you decide what to seal?

We inspect inside and out, mark gaps a quarter inch or wider, and prioritize those with droppings, rub marks or gnawing nearby.

Do you do carpentry or masonry?

We handle sealing and basic closures. Structural repair may need a contractor, and we will tell you where.

Will sealing end the problem by itself?

Only if no rodents are left inside. That is why trapping and follow-up come with sealing.

How long does sealing last?

Metal, copper wool and caulk hold up well, but buildings shift, so we recommend an annual look at the exterior.

Do you do exclusion or only trapping?

Both. Exclusion is described on this page and trapping on our rat and mouse control pages. Most jobs use both.

Can you seal and decontaminate where they got in?

We seal the entry points and clean affected surfaces using CDC wet-cleaning steps. Heavily soiled materials may need to be removed.

Do you re-check the work later?

We offer maintenance plans that include checking sealed areas and traps. Ask about one when you book.

What can I change outside to help?

Extension advises removing leaves, brush piles, trash and woodpiles near the house, and sealing up garbage cans, birdseed, livestock feed and pet food. Less shelter and food outside means fewer animals testing your foundation.
