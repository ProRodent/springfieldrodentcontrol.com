Insulation Replacement After Rodent Damage in Springfield, MO

Insulation soaked with urine and full of droppings will not insulate well and will keep smelling. Removing it is often the fix.

Call (844) 635-0403 Contact Us

Rodents tunnel through insulation, nest in it and soil it. Damaged insulation loses its value, and contamination can remain even after the animals are gone.

When insulation needs to go

Matted, stained, urine-soaked or dropping-filled insulation is better removed than cleaned. Lightly affected spots may be spot-treated, but we make that call only after looking at the whole attic.

Safe removal

CDC advises against disturbing droppings and nesting materials in a way that raises dust. We wet down the material, wear protective gear and bag it for disposal before any new insulation goes in.

Re-insulating after sealing

New insulation should only go in after the roofline and vents are sealed and trapping is finished. Otherwise it becomes new nesting material. We coordinate the order of work so you do not pay for the same attic twice.

Insulation work steps

  • Confirm animals are gone and entry points are sealed
  • Wet down and remove contaminated insulation
  • Sanitize framing and exposed surfaces
  • Install new insulation or arrange for an insulation contractor
  • Final check for new activity

If this matches what you are seeing, it may also help to read about attic rodent cleanup and sanitization, proofing the attic and sealing the crawl space.

FAQs About Insulation Replacement After Rodent Damage in Springfield

Do you install new insulation?

We can remove and replace insulation, or we can hand off to an insulation contractor you choose. Ask when you call.

Why not just add new insulation over the old?

Contaminated material underneath still smells and can harbor droppings. Removal is usually the better route.

How do I know if insulation is damaged?

Look for flattened areas, tunnels, stains, droppings and shredded material.

Is there a health risk?

Rodent waste can contaminate surfaces. CDC recommends wet cleanup and gloves, and avoiding sweeping or vacuuming.
